# Calculus II — an expedition through accumulation

This is a rebuilt course design, not a conversion of the archived eight-lesson collection. Only the first new investigation is published initially. The earlier collection retains its permanent IDs and visit history.

## Governing question
When can a whole be reconstructed from local contributions, and how can we justify the result when the reconstruction is indirect, approximate, or infinite?

## How the course develops
A question arrives before its complete explanation. An original Nomai dialogue exposes competing theories; supplied records, diagrams, calculations, and small tools make those theories testable. Return to a provisional map and distinguish what follows from evidence, what follows from an assumed model, and what remains unknown. Familiarity with a name is different from being able to explain its relationships.

The territories below cover a full college Calculus II scope. They are a navigable map, not mandatory browsing gates or a fixed number of sessions. Publish bounded investigations as their questions become useful; do not publish empty lesson shells or reserve speculative IDs.

| Territory | Questions that drive investigation | Mathematical mechanisms | Evidence of usable understanding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reconstructing a whole | How can a few local readings support a total? What changes as pieces become smaller? | Riemann sums, definite integrals, signed accumulation, partitions, integrability, units | Construct the contribution being added; bound a model's total; distinguish model assumptions from observations |
| Recovering exact answers | Why does an antiderivative evaluate a total? What information disappears on differentiation? | Accumulation functions, Fundamental Theorem, initial conditions, substitution, logarithmic and inverse-trigonometric forms | Derive an integral from a system, choose a representation, verify by differentiation and units |
| Changing representation | Which part of an expression is making the problem difficult? | Integration by parts, trigonometric identities and substitution, partial fractions, method selection | Explain a useful first move; compare alternatives; preserve domains and endpoints; check a reconstructed answer |
| Geometry and distributed quantities | What is a slice of this object? Why does the same object lead to different integrals? | Area between curves, disks/washers, shells, arc length, surface area, density, average value, work, centers of mass | Choose a small contribution and derive its geometry, rather than match an object to a memorized formula |
| Finite computation and unbounded domains | How accurate is this estimate? Can an unbounded process have a finite total? | Midpoint/trapezoidal/Simpson methods, numerical error versus data uncertainty, improper integrals, comparison and limiting procedures | Give a justified bound when available; identify assumptions needed for a bound; separate convergence from cancellation |
| Discrete accumulation | Does a shrinking contribution guarantee a settling total? | Sequences, partial sums, geometric and telescoping series, divergence test, integral/comparison/ratio/root tests, absolute and conditional convergence | Construct counterexamples; select and justify a test; distinguish term behavior from total behavior; estimate a tail |
| Local models of complicated functions | How much of a function can local information reconstruct, and where does that model fail? | Power series, convergence intervals and endpoints, Taylor/Maclaurin polynomials and series, remainders, termwise operations under their conditions | Build an approximation for a purpose, state its region of validity, and justify an error tolerance |
| Curves beyond y=f(x) | What changes when a path has its own clock or a different coordinate system? | Parametric curves, derivatives and arc length, polar coordinates and area, return visits to approximation and accumulation | Distinguish a path from its traversal; choose coordinates that simplify a contribution; connect geometric and analytic descriptions |

## First published investigation
`a-mass-between-readings` — **What lies between the readings?** Three density measurements invite a linear theory. Finite slices yield two different mass estimates. Refining the partition leads to a limit; a hidden triangular contribution then shows why numerical certainty inside a model does not establish the model itself.

## Natural continuations
- Move the upper endpoint: can the accumulated mass reveal the local density? This opens accumulation functions and the Fundamental Theorem.
- Change the density to a curved function: does midpoint sampling remain exact? This opens error and numerical integration.
- Ask where the object balances: why must location weight each mass contribution? This opens moments and center of mass.

These are possible questions, not claims of published lessons. Formal cross-lesson links need existing permanent endpoints.

## Activity and feedback design
Every required task includes data and a means of doing the work. In-line probes invite provisional theories before explanations; end activities ask for reconstruction, a changed coordinate, and a counterexample. Optional feedback gives reasoning, not merely numbers. The five feedback measures stay separate; no rating or elapsed time is treated as proof of mastery. Estimate the actual reading, investigation, setup, and activity burden without duration bounds.

## Preservation
The archive is readable and excluded from the current path. New lessons receive new permanent IDs. An uncertain or revised theory is retained in visit history; later understanding supplies context without rewriting earlier records.
